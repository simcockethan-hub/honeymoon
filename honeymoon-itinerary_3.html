Tue 1 – Wed 2 Sep · Days 2–3

Two full city days — spend one wisely

On footCastle · market · Metelkova · Tivoli

Ljubljana genuinely only needs one full day: castle, central market (morning), Metelkova's murals, Tivoli Park at golden hour. Which frees a decision:

The choice

Day-trip on day 3, or save it? Bled is 45 minutes away and the obvious lure — but you drive right past it on Thursday anyway. Recommendation: don't burn a day on it now. Do Postojna/Predjama Castle as the day-3 trip if you want one (1h away, castle-in-a-cliff), or take the rest day. You'll want the fuel later.

Thu 3 Sep · Day 4

Into the Alps — via the postcard

Ljubljana → Bled → Bohinj~80 km1h15 driving + stops

Checkout, then the route earns its keep: Vintgar Gorge first (boardwalks over glacial water — go at opening, before the buses), then Lake Bled — pletna boat to the island or just the lakeside walk, and a kremšnita cream cake at the castle view. That's 3–4 unhurried hours.

Then 25 more minutes and the crowds simply vanish: Lake Bohinj is Bled's bigger, wilder, quieter sibling. Check in, swim if the sun's out, sunset at the Church of St John bridge.

Make it epicBled by 10am means island + cake before lunch and an empty Bohinj beach by 4pm. The whole day is a warm-up act structured around one truth: sleep by the quiet lake, visit the famous one.
Fri 4 Sep · Day 5

The mountain day

Bohinj basinVogel · Savica · the lake

One full Julian Alps day, weather-dependent by design: if it's clear, Vogel cable car first — the Triglav panorama from the top station is the single best view of the trip's first week. Then Savica Waterfall (25-min hike), then the afternoon on the water — canoe or SUP rental at Ribčev Laz, or the swim you'll describe for years.

Weather call

Clouds down? Flip the day: Mostnica Gorge (all-weather, gorgeous) and the Alpine Dairy Museum, save the splurge. Vogel with no view is money on fog.

Sat 5 Sep · Day 6

Over — or under — to the Soča

Bohinj → Tolmin~50 km1h15 over the hillsor: the car train

Short move, big character, and a genuine either/or:

Road or rail

(a) Drive Baška Grapa — 1h15 of tight, pretty valley switchbacks. (b) The avtovlak: at Bohinjska Bistrica you drive the Tesla-less Golf onto a flatbed rail car and ride inside your own car through a 6-km WWI-era tunnel to Most na Soči — 40 minutes, a few euros, one of Europe's last car-shuttle trains. Check Saturday departure times the day before. It's the quirkier story.

Either way you surface in the Soča valley by lunch. Afternoon: Tolmin Gorges — the confluence, the Devil's Bridge, water an impossible blue-green. Then check in at Camp Šiber (cash, at reception) and grill something under the trees.

Make it epicTake the train in, and detour 25 min up-valley to Kobarid for lunch — the WWI museum is world-class and Hiša Franko's casual sister restaurant is in town if you want one big-deal meal in Slovenia.
Sun 6 Sep · Day 7

Mountains to salt water in one morning

Tolmin → Izola~110 km~1h50+ Škocjan detour

The day the trip changes texture. Wind down the Soča, across the Karst, and the one stop worth the pause: Škocjan Caves — UNESCO, a 90-minute walk through a canyon inside the earth. Guided entries run on the hour; check morning times. (Predjama Castle is the lighter alternative if caves aren't the mood.)

Then: the Adriatic. First swim off Izola's town beach, then the move that makes the evening — Piran is 15 minutes away. Tartini Square, the walls at sunset, seafood on the water. Sleep back in cheaper, calmer Izola.

Check: Škocjan tour times
Mon 7 Sep · Day 8

One border, one hill town, then Rovinj

Izola → Rovinj~75 km direct1h15 + border+1h with Motovun

The only border of the whole drive — Slovenia into Croatia at Dragonja. Passports in the door pocket; Monday morning queues are usually short. Both countries are Schengen and euro now, so it's a wave-through on a good day.

The detour

September is truffle season in Istria. Swinging inland through Motovun (or quieter Grožnjan) adds an hour and gets you a medieval hilltop town and a truffle-pasta lunch that will bully every meal for a week. Strongly endorsed.

Into Rovinj by late afternoon — the Airbnb has free parking, which in Rovinj is worth its weight in truffles. Old town at dusk: Grisia street up to St Euphemia's, batana boats in the harbour.

Tue 8 Sep · Day 9

Rovinj, properly

Rovinj + Zlatni Rtoptional: Pula, 40 min

A no-driving day if you want one: morning swim and shaded cycling in Golden Cape (Zlatni Rt) forest park — pebble coves five minutes from the old town — long lunch, slow afternoon.

Optional add

Pula's Roman amphitheatre is 40 minutes south — one of the six largest ever built, and you walk the arena floor. Doable as a 3-hour round trip; skip if the beach is winning.

Make it epicDinner reservation tonight matters more than any other Rovinj call — the water-edge terraces book out. Ask the host for their one pick the day you arrive.
Wed 9 Sep · Day 10

The long haul — built around one great stop

Rovinj → Omiš≈490 km5h45 drivingA1 motorway

The one genuinely big day, and the reason Omiš exists on this plan at all — a bed placed exactly where the driving should stop. The A1 is fast, empty, and scenic in its own inland way. The play is one anchor stop, not three:

Pick one

Zadar (on route, +30 min): lunch in the old town, the Sea Organ playing the harbour — best value per minute. Plitvice Lakes (+2h detour, 3h inside): the fairy-tale one, but it turns this into a 10-hour day and needs a 7am start plus a timed ticket. Krka/Šibenik (+1h): the compromise. Honest recommendation: Zadar lunch, then push on.

Roll past Split (save it — see tomorrow) and into Omiš by early evening: a fortress town wedged into a literal canyon mouth where the Cetina river hits the sea. Hotel Marina, €10 parking, dinner, done.

If Plitvice: timed entry + 7am start
Thu 10 Sep · Day 11

The coast road, the bridge, the boat

Omiš → Orebić~185 km~3h driving+ 20-min car ferry

The most beautiful driving day of the trip, no contest. First, if you're up early: the Fortica fortress above Omiš (45-min climb, canyon-and-sea view) — or the Cetina canyon zipline, which needs booking days ahead.

Then the Makarska Riviera — the D8 coastal road with Biokovo's 1,700-metre wall on your left and that impossible water on your right. Pull-offs at Brela (the famous stone) are mandatory. Vertigo fans: the Biokovo Skywalk detour adds 1.5h.

Boat choice

At Ploče you choose: (a) Pelješac Bridge — the elegant 2022 span, free, no waiting, then the peninsula road to Orebić; or (b) Ploče–Trpanj car ferry — 1h on the water, cuts driving, but sails to a timetable. The bridge is faster and more reliable; the ferry is more romantic. Either way it ends the same: Orebić → Dominče car ferry, 20 minutes across to Korčula, then 10 minutes to Lumbarda.

Make it epicThe Pelješac road runs straight through Dingač wine country — Croatia's best reds, grown on slopes so steep they built a tunnel for the grapes. A 4pm cellar stop (Matuško or Grgić, book a tasting) with Brooke, 30 minutes before the ferry, is the honeymoon photo.
Book: zipline (if wanted)Book: Pelješac tasting ~4pm
Fri 11 – Sat 12 Sep · Days 12–13

Korčula — the exhale

Lumbarda baseGrk vineyards on footOld town 15 min away

Two clear days, zero obligations. The Dreamers' Club sits in Lumbarda's vineyard flats, and this is the one place on earth that grows Grk — a white that ripens only in this sand. Bire and Zure wineries are walking distance from the room. Zure also does a family-table dinner worth booking.

Split the days: one for Vela Pržina beach (sandy — rare here — 15 min walk) and lazy vineyard wandering; one for Korčula Town — a miniature Dubrovnik without the crowds, Marco Polo's alleged tower, swimming off the rocks at Banje. Water-taxi to Badija island (monastery + tame deer) if a boat urge strikes.

Make it epicSaturday sunset: the west-facing beach bars in Lumbarda point directly at the sun dropping behind Pelješac's ridge. Book nothing after 6pm. That's the whole plan.
Book: Zure dinner
Sun 13 Sep · Day 14

Oysters, walls, and the last miles

Ferry backOrebić → Ston → Dubrovnik~150 km~3h + stops

Ferry off the island, back along Pelješac — and this time stop at Ston: Europe's longest fortress walls (a 40-minute rampart walk if it's not scorching) and, in Mali Ston, the oyster beds that have fed Dubrovnik for 500 years. Lunch here is non-negotiable. Fun link: Bota Šare in Mali Ston is the original of the Bota on Brooke's Dubrovnik list — same family, same oysters.

Then the D8 south — all in Croatia, the bridge means no Bosnia corridor — with one soft stop at Trsteno Arboretum (Renaissance gardens over the sea, 30 min). Into Dubrovnik by late afternoon: park at Ilijina Glavica garage (or as agreed at the Enterprise counter), roll bags 10 minutes to Hotel Stari Grad inside the walls.

Make it epicFirst evening ritual: through Pile Gate at golden hour, straight down Stradun, out the hole in the seaward wall to Buža Bar — a drink on the rocks with the sun going down on day 14. It's on her list. Start there.
Mon 14 – Tue 15 Sep · Days 15–16

Dubrovnik — everything is six minutes away

Hotel Stari Grad, in the wallsDubravka booked Mon 6:30pm

The map is zoomed all the way in for a reason: every one of Brooke's nine restaurants sits within a 6-minute walk of the hotel door. Dubravka and Nautika cluster at Pile Gate, Proto and Lucin Kantun are practically on your street, Arsenal and Bota flank the old port, Buža hangs off the seaward wall. Zero logistics. Pure choosing.

The two mornings: walls at 08:00 sharp Monday (tickets in hand, 90 minutes, before heat and cruise crowds), then Lokrum island Tuesday — 15-min boat from the old port, swimming coves, peacocks, back by mid-afternoon. Srđ cable car for one sunset. Monday 6:30pm: Dubravka 1836 — booked, table held only 15 minutes, city walls glowing over your shoulder.
